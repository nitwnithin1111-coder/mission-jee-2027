Pillar 1

Newton's Law, Symmetry Systems & Mutual Orbits

💡 The Intuitive Story: The Shyest Force in the Universe

Gravity is the weakest fundamental force in nature. Two 1 kg iron balls placed 1 meter apart attract each other with only $6.67 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}$—the weight of a single bacterium. Gravity only becomes formidable because it is strictly attractive (unlike electromagnetism, there is no negative charge to cancel it out). As masses accumulate into planets and stars, this tiny force becomes an unyielding cosmic grip.

$$F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}, \quad G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$$

🎯 Key High-Yield Symmetry Patterns:

1. Particles Moving in a Mutual Gravitational Circle:

When two equal masses $m$ revolve in a circle of radius $r$ under mutual gravity, the distance between them is the diameter $2r$. $$\text{Gravitational Force} = \frac{Gm^2}{(2r)^2} = \frac{Gm^2}{4r^2}$$ $$\text{Centripetal Force} = \frac{mv^2}{r} \implies \frac{Gm^2}{4r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r} \implies \mathbf{v = \sqrt{\frac{Gm}{4r}}}$$

⚠️ Trap: Forgetting that the separation distance is $2r$ instead of $r$ is the classic negative-mark trap!

2. The Cavity / "Negative Mass" Technique:

When a scoop of radius $R/3$ is cut out of a solid sphere of mass $M$, never try to integrate the weird remaining shape! Instead, treat the leftover sphere as: $$\mathbf{\text{Remaining Force}} = \mathbf{\text{Force of Full Sphere}} - \mathbf{\text{Force of the Cut-out Scoop}}$$ Mass of cut-out scoop: Since volume $\propto r^3$, mass of scoop $m_{\text{scoop}} = M \times \left(\frac{R/3}{R}\right)^3 = \frac{M}{27}$.

Pillar 2 (28% of all JEE Questions)

Acceleration Due to Gravity ($g$) & Its 4 Variations

💡 The Intuitive Story: Earth's Gravitational Peak

Acceleration due to gravity is maximum on Earth's surface ($g \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$). As you climb into space, you get farther from the mass, so $g$ drops inversely with the square of distance. As you dig into a mine toward the center, the outer shell of Earth pulls you in all directions equally (net zero force), so only the smaller inner sphere pulls you, causing $g$ to drop linearly to zero at the core.

1. At Height $h$ (General):

$$g_h = \frac{g}{\left(1 + \frac{h}{R}\right)^2}$$

Use this whenever $h$ is large ($h = R, 2R, R/2$).

2. At Height $h$ (Approximation for $h \ll R$):

$$g_h \approx g\left(1 - \frac{2h}{R}\right)$$

Use only when $h \le 300\text{ km}$ ($h/R \le 5\%$).

3. At Depth $d$ Below Surface:

$$g_d = g\left(1 - \frac{d}{R}\right)$$

Exact at ANY depth! At center ($d=R$), $g_d = 0$.

4. Rotation Effect at Latitude $\lambda$:

$$g' = g - \omega^2 R \cos^2\lambda$$

Equator ($\lambda=0$): $g_{\text{eq}} = g - \omega^2 R$ (Min). Pole ($\lambda=90^\circ$): $g_{\text{pole}} = g$ (Max).

🔥 The #1 Repeated NTA Question Archetype:

"At what depth $d$ is gravity equal to that at a height $h$?"

  • Case A (When $h \ll R$, e.g. $16\text{ km}$ or $32\text{ km}$):
    $1 - \frac{d}{R} = 1 - \frac{2h}{R} \implies \mathbf{d = 2h}$. (Depth must be twice the height!)
  • Case B (When $h = R$ or $h$ is large):
    You MUST use the exact formula! For $h = R$: $g_h = \frac{g}{(1+1)^2} = \frac{g}{4}$.
    $1 - \frac{d}{R} = \frac{1}{4} \implies \mathbf{d = \frac{3}{4}R}$.
Pillar 3

Gravitational Potential & Potential Energy

💡 The Intuitive Story: Why is Potential Energy Negative?

Think of infinity ($\infty$) as zero debt—you are completely free from Earth's gravity ($U=0$). As you fall toward Earth, gravity does positive work, meaning you fall into a gravitational debt pit. That is why potential energy is negative: $U = -\frac{GMm}{r}$. To climb out of the well and escape forever, you must pay back that energy.

The Golden Formula for Raising a Mass to Height $h$:

$$\Delta U = U_f - U_i = \mathbf{\frac{mgh}{1 + \frac{h}{R}}}$$

If $h \ll R$: $\Delta U \approx mgh$
If $h = R$: $\Delta U = \frac{1}{2}mgR$
If $h = 2R$: $\Delta U = \frac{2}{3}mgR$
Solid Sphere Potential (Inside vs Outside):

• At surface: $V_{\text{surface}} = -\frac{GM}{R}$
• At center: $V_{\text{center}} = -\frac{3}{2}\frac{GM}{R} = \mathbf{1.5 \times V_{\text{surface}}}$ (The deepest point of the pit!)

Pillar 4

Escape Velocity ($v_e$) & Planet Ratios

💡 The Intuitive Story: Buying a One-Way Ticket to Infinity

Escape velocity is the minimum launch speed where kinetic energy completely cancels your negative potential energy debt ($K + U = 0$). Once you have this speed, you coast all the way to infinity with zero fuel, arriving with exactly zero velocity.

$$v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{2gR} = \mathbf{R \sqrt{\frac{8\pi G \rho}{3}}}$$

For Earth: $v_e \approx 11.2\text{ km/s}$

When Given Mass & Radius Ratio:

$$v_e \propto \sqrt{\frac{M}{R}}$$

When Given Density & Radius Ratio:

$$v_e \propto R \sqrt{\rho}$$

Pillar 5

Satellite Motion, The Energy Triangle & Kepler's Laws

💡 The Intuitive Story: Falling Forever and Never Landing

An orbiting satellite is in continuous free fall. It is dropping toward Earth at $9.8\text{ m/s}^2$, but it is moving horizontally so fast ($\approx 7.9\text{ km/s}$) that as it falls, the curved surface of Earth drops away beneath it at the exact same rate. The satellite falls forever around the bend.

The Sacred Energy Triangle of an Orbiting Satellite:
Kinetic Energy (Positive): $$K = +\frac{GMm}{2r}$$
Potential Energy (Negative): $$U = -\frac{GMm}{r}$$
Total Energy (Negative): $$E = -\frac{GMm}{2r}$$
$$\mathbf{U = 2E}, \quad \mathbf{K = -E}, \quad \mathbf{E = -K = \frac{U}{2}}$$
Kepler's Law (Variable Star Mass):

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM_*}} \implies T^2 \propto \frac{r^3}{M_*}$$

Remember: If star mass doubles, period drops by $\sqrt{2}$.

Extra Velocity to Escape from Low Orbit:

$$\Delta v = v_e - v_o = (\sqrt{2} - 1)\sqrt{gR}$$

Numerically: $\Delta v \approx 8(\sqrt{2}-1)\text{ km/s} \approx 3.3\text{ km/s}$.

📍 Category A: Variations of $g$ & Potential Energy at Heights

Q1 • JEE Main 2026 (5th April Evening Shift) Correct: (D) $\frac{2}{3}mgR_e$

A body of mass $m$ is taken from the surface of earth to a height equal to twice the radius of earth ($R_e$). The increase in potential energy will be:

(A) $\frac{1}{2}mgR_e$
(B) $\frac{3}{4}mgR_e$
(C) $\frac{1}{3}mgR_e$
(D) $\frac{2}{3}mgR_e$
💡 Step-by-Step Derivation:

Initial PE at surface ($r_i = R_e$): $U_i = -\frac{GM_e m}{R_e}$
Final PE at height $h = 2R_e$ ($r_f = R_e + 2R_e = 3R_e$): $U_f = -\frac{GM_e m}{3R_e}$
$$\Delta U = U_f - U_i = -\frac{GM_e m}{3R_e} - \left(-\frac{GM_e m}{R_e}\right) = \frac{GM_e m}{R_e}\left(1 - \frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{2}{3}\frac{GM_e m}{R_e}$$ Since $g = \frac{GM_e}{R_e^2} \implies \frac{GM_e m}{R_e} = mgR_e$, we get: $$\mathbf{\Delta U = \frac{2}{3}mgR_e}$$

⚡ 10-Second Shortcut: Directly use $\Delta U = \frac{mgh}{1 + h/R}$. Plug in $h=2R \implies \frac{mg(2R)}{1+2} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3}mgR}$. Done in 5 seconds!

Q2 • JEE Main 2026 (5th April Morning Shift) Correct: (B) 0.25

When one moves from a point 16 km below the earth's surface to a point 16 km above the earth's surface, the change in $g$ is approximately $\alpha\%$. The value of $\alpha$ is: (Take radius of the earth = 6400 km.)

(A) 0.12
(B) 0.25
(C) 0.50
(D) 0.75
💡 Step-by-Step Derivation:

Here $h = d = 16\text{ km} \ll R$ ($6400\text{ km}$).
Gravity at depth $d$: $g_{\text{below}} = g\left(1 - \frac{d}{R}\right)$
Gravity at height $h$: $g_{\text{above}} \approx g\left(1 - \frac{2h}{R}\right)$
Change in $g$: $\Delta g = g_{\text{below}} - g_{\text{above}} = g\left[\left(1 - \frac{d}{R}\right) - \left(1 - \frac{2h}{R}\right)\right] = g\left(\frac{2h - d}{R}\right)$
Since $h = d = 16\text{ km}$: $$\Delta g = g\frac{16}{6400} = \frac{g}{400}$$ Percentage change: $\alpha\% = \frac{\Delta g}{g} \times 100 = \frac{1}{400} \times 100 = \mathbf{0.25\%}$.

Q3 • JEE Main 2026 (4th April Evening Shift) Correct: (C) $2R$

The height in terms of radius of the earth ($R$), at which the acceleration due to gravity becomes $g/9$, where $g$ is acceleration due to gravity on earth's surface, is:

(A) $\sqrt{3}R$
(B) $2\sqrt{2}R$
(C) $2R$
(D) $\frac{4}{9}R$
💡 Step-by-Step Derivation:

$$g_h = \frac{g}{\left(1 + \frac{h}{R}\right)^2} = \frac{g}{9}$$ $$\left(1 + \frac{h}{R}\right)^2 = 9 \implies 1 + \frac{h}{R} = 3 \implies \frac{h}{R} = 2 \implies \mathbf{h = 2R}$$

⚠️ Trap: If a student wrongly used the linear formula $g(1-2h/R) = g/9 \implies 2h/R = 8/9 \implies h = 4R/9$, they would have picked option (D) and lost 5 marks!

📍 Category B: Kepler's 3rd Law & Orbital Periods

Q5 • JEE Main 2026 (2nd April Morning Shift) Correct: (B) 2

A planet ($P_1$) is moving around a star of mass $2M$ in an orbit of radius $R$. Another planet ($P_2$) is moving around another star of mass $4M$ in an orbit of radius $2R$. The ratio of time periods of revolution of $P_2$ and $P_1$ is:

(A) 1/2
(B) 2
(C) 4
(D) 1/4
💡 Step-by-Step Derivation:

From gravitational centripetal balance: $\frac{G M_* m}{r^2} = m\omega^2 r = m\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}\right)^2 r$
$$T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2 r^3}{G M_*} \implies T \propto \sqrt{\frac{r^3}{M_*}}$$ $$\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \sqrt{\frac{r_2^3}{r_1^3} \times \frac{M_{*1}}{M_{*2}}} = \sqrt{\frac{(2R)^3}{R^3} \times \frac{2M}{4M}} = \sqrt{8 \times \frac{1}{2}} = \sqrt{4} = \mathbf{2}$$

Q10 • JEE Main 2023 (29th Jan Evening Shift) Correct: (D) 3 hours

The time period of a satellite of earth is 24 hours. If the separation between the earth and the satellite is decreased to one fourth of the previous value, then its new time period will become:

(A) 4 hours
(B) 6 hours
(C) 12 hours
(D) 3 hours
💡 Step-by-Step Derivation:

$$T^2 \propto r^3 \implies \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\frac{r_2}{r_1}\right)^{3/2}$$ Here $r_2 = \frac{r_1}{4}$: $$\frac{T_2}{24} = \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{3/2} = \left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2\right]^{3/2} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{1}{8}$$ $$T_2 = \frac{24}{8} = \mathbf{3\text{ hours}}$$

📍 Category C: Escape Velocity & Parameter Ratios

Q8 • JEE Main 2026 (22nd Jan Morning Shift) Correct: (D) $100\sqrt{10}\text{ m/s}$

The escape velocity from a spherical planet A is $10\text{ km/s}$. The escape velocity from another planet B whose density and radius are $10\%$ of those of planet A, is:

(A) 1000 m/s
(B) $200\sqrt{5}\text{ m/s}$
(C) $1000\sqrt{2}\text{ m/s}$
(D) $100\sqrt{10}\text{ m/s}$
💡 Step-by-Step Derivation:

Escape velocity in terms of density: $$v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{\frac{2G(\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 \rho)}{R}} = R \sqrt{\frac{8\pi G \rho}{3}} \implies \mathbf{v_e \propto R\sqrt{\rho}}$$ $$\frac{v_B}{v_A} = \frac{R_B}{R_A} \sqrt{\frac{\rho_B}{\rho_A}} = \left(\frac{1}{10}\right) \sqrt{\frac{1}{10}} = \frac{1}{10\sqrt{10}}$$ $$v_B = \frac{10\text{ km/s}}{10\sqrt{10}} = \frac{10 \times 10^3\text{ m/s}}{10\sqrt{10}} = \frac{1000}{\sqrt{10}} = \frac{1000\sqrt{10}}{10} = \mathbf{100\sqrt{10}\text{ m/s}}$$

📍 Category D: Orbital Energy Transitions

Q10 • JEE Main 2026 (21st Jan Morning Shift) Correct: (B) 1000

Initially a satellite of $100\text{ kg}$ is in a circular orbit of radius $1.5R_E$. This satellite can be moved to a circular orbit of radius $3R_E$ by supplying $\alpha \times 10^6\text{ J}$ of energy. The value of $\alpha$ is: (Take Radius of Earth $R_E = 6 \times 10^6\text{ m}$ and $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$)

(A) 500
(B) 1000
(C) 100
(D) 150
💡 Step-by-Step Derivation:

Total energy of a satellite in orbit of radius $r$: $E = -\frac{GM_E m}{2r}$
Energy required: $$\Delta E = E_f - E_i = -\frac{GM_E m}{2(3R_E)} - \left(-\frac{GM_E m}{2(1.5R_E)}\right) = \frac{GM_E m}{2R_E}\left(\frac{1}{1.5} - \frac{1}{3}\right)$$ Notice $\frac{1}{1.5} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{3}$. $$\Delta E = \frac{GM_E m}{2R_E}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{GM_E m}{6R_E} = \frac{mgR_E}{6}$$ Now plug in the values: $$\Delta E = \frac{100 \times 10 \times (6 \times 10^6)}{6} = 100 \times 10 \times 10^6 = \mathbf{1000 \times 10^6\text{ J}}$$ Therefore, $\mathbf{\alpha = 1000}$.
