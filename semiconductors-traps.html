TRAP 01 • MOST FREQUENT IN JEE 2026
Zener Diode with Input Voltage Below Breakdown ($V_{in} < V_Z$)
🛑 What the Student Does (Wrong → -1 Mark)
A problem states: "A Zener diode of breakdown voltage $V_Z = 10\text{ V}$ is placed in a regulator circuit with $R_S = 1\text{ k}\Omega$ and $R_L = 1\text{ k}\Omega$. If $V_{in} = 8\text{ V}$, find voltage across $R_L$."
Student reflexively writes: $V_L = 10\text{ V}$ because "it's a Zener regulator!" (How can output be 10V when power supply is only 8V?!).
✅ What NTA Expects (Right Approach)
The Zener diode conducts ONLY IF $V_{in} \ge V_Z$ across its terminals.
If $V_{in} < V_Z$, the Zener is OFF ($I_Z = 0$), acting as an OPEN CIRCUIT.
The circuit becomes a simple resistive voltage divider: $$V_L = V_{in} \left(\frac{R_L}{R_S + R_L}\right) = 8 \text{ V} \times \left(\frac{1}{1 + 1}\right) = 4\text{ V}$$ Always check if the unregulated input exceeds $V_Z$ before clamping to $V_Z$!
TRAP 02 • THEORETICAL FAVORITE
Why Photodiode Operates in Reverse Bias (Not Forward Bias)
🛑 What the Student Does (Wrong)
Student reasons: "In forward bias, current is in milliamperes, which is large and easily detectable, so photodiode should operate in forward bias."
✅ What NTA Expects (Right Approach)
Let majority carrier density be $10^{16}$ and minority be $10^8$.
When illuminated, suppose optical generation produces $\Delta n = 10^7$ carriers:
• Forward bias (majority carriers): Fractional increase is $\frac{10^7}{10^{16}} = 10^{-9}$ (utterly undetectable!).
• Reverse bias (minority carriers): Fractional increase is $\frac{10^7}{10^8} = 10\% = 0.1$ (massive, immediately measurable!).
Hence, photodiodes are strictly operated in reverse bias for maximum optical sensitivity.
TRAP 03 • POLARITY CONFUSION
Identifying Forward vs Reverse Bias with Negative Terminal Potentials
🛑 What the Student Does (Wrong)
A diode has p-side at $-4\text{ V}$ and n-side at $-8\text{ V}$.
Student sees negative voltages on both sides and marks "Reverse biased" because "negative sign means reverse bias".
✅ What NTA Expects (Right Approach)
Bias condition depends strictly on relative potential difference $V_p - V_n$:
$$\text{Forward Biased} \iff V_p > V_n$$ Here, $V_p = -4\text{ V}$ and $V_n = -8\text{ V}$.
Since $-4\text{ V} > -8\text{ V}$, the p-side is at a HIGHER potential than the n-side ($\Delta V = +4\text{ V}$).
Therefore, the diode is FORWARD BIASED and conducts current!
TRAP 04 • RECTIFIER RIPPLE FREQUENCY
Full-Wave vs Half-Wave Rectifier AC Ripple Frequency
🛑 What the Student Does (Wrong)
"If input mains frequency is 50 Hz, what is the ripple frequency in full-wave rectifier output?"
Student answers: 50 Hz.
✅ What NTA Expects (Right Approach)
• In Half-Wave Rectifier: Only 1 pulse per cycle $\implies f_{ripple} = f_{in} = 50\text{ Hz}$.
• In Full-Wave Rectifier: Both positive and negative half-cycles are folded into positive peaks $\implies 2$ pulses per cycle $\implies f_{ripple} = 2 f_{in} = 2 \times 50 = \mathbf{100\text{ Hz}}$.
TRAP 05 • SOLAR CELL GRAPH
Operating Quadrant of a Solar Cell $I\text{-}V$ Characteristic
🛑 What the Student Does (Wrong)
Student draws the solar cell curve in the 1st quadrant like a standard diode or LED because "it's a semiconductor device."
✅ What NTA Expects (Right Approach)
A solar cell does not consume electrical power; it GENERATES electrical power to an external load.
In generator convention, current flows OUT of the positive terminal ($I < 0$ while $V > 0$).
Hence, the characteristic curve lies exclusively in the FOURTH QUADRANT of the Cartesian plane!
