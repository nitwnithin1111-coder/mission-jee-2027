Gravitation

Physics Master Guide Target JEE Main 2027 Formulas • Derivations • Verified PYQs

Gravitation describes the attractive interaction between masses in a central, conservative inverse-square force field. Based on our empirical analysis of 219 verified JEE Main questions (2018–2026), this chapter consistently yields 1 to 2 questions per exam shift, concentrating heavily on orbital dynamics, variations of surface gravity, and escape velocity.

1. Newton's Universal Law & Gravitational Field

Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a central force directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance separating their centers.

The mutual attractive force acting along the line of centers is expressed vectorially as:
Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation
$$\mathbf{F} = - G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} \hat{\mathbf{r}}$$
  • $\mathbf{F}$ : Gravitational attractive force vector $(\text{N})$
  • $G$ : Universal gravitational constant $(\approx 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2)$
  • $m_1, m_2$ : Interacting point masses $(\text{kg})$
  • $r$ : Distance between centers of mass $(\text{m})$
  • $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ : Unit vector directed from one mass towards the other
The gravitational field intensity represents the gravitational force experienced per unit test mass placed in the field:
Gravitational Field Intensity ($\mathbf{E}$)
$$\mathbf{E} = \frac{\mathbf{F}}{m_0} = - \frac{G M}{r^2} \hat{\mathbf{r}}$$
  • $\mathbf{E}$ : Gravitational field intensity vector $(\text{N/kg}\text{ or }\text{m/s}^2)$
  • $M$ : Source mass creating the field $(\text{kg})$
  • $m_0$ : Small test mass $(\text{kg})$
Mass Distribution Location Gravitational Field ($\mathbf{E}$) Variation with Radius ($r$)
Point Mass / Outside Sphere $r \ge R$ $E = \frac{G M}{r^2}$ $E \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$ (Inverse Square)
Solid Sphere (Inside) $r \le R$ $E = \frac{G M r}{R^3}$ $E \propto r$ (Directly Linear)
Spherical Shell (Inside) $r < R$ $E = 0$ Uniformly Zero Field

2. Acceleration Due to Gravity ($g$) & Variations

At the surface of a spherical planet of mass $M$, radius $R$, and mean density $\rho$, the gravitational acceleration experienced by a body is derived from Newton's second law:

Expressing mass in terms of planetary volume and uniform density gives:
Surface Acceleration Formula
$$g = \frac{G M}{R^2} = \frac{4}{3} \pi G \rho R \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$$
  • $g$ : Acceleration due to gravity at surface $(\text{m/s}^2)$
  • $R$ : Planetary radius $(\text{Earth: } R \approx 6400\text{ km})$
  • $\rho$ : Mean mass density of the planet $(\text{kg/m}^3)$
Variation with Altitude ($h$ Above Surface)

As an object moves away from the surface to an altitude $h$, the distance from the Earth's center becomes $(R + h)$.

The exact scaling relation valid for all altitudes is:
Exact Gravity at Altitude $h$
$$g_h = g \left(\frac{R}{R + h}\right)^2 = \frac{g}{\left(1 + \frac{h}{R}\right)^2}$$
  • $g_h$ : Acceleration due to gravity at altitude $h$ $(\text{m/s}^2)$
  • $h$ : Height above planetary surface $(\text{m})$
When $h \ll R$ ($h < 300\text{ km}$), expanding via the binomial theorem yields the first-order approximation:
Small Height Approximation ($h \ll R$)
$$g_h \approx g \left(1 - \frac{2h}{R}\right)$$
  • Condition : Valid only when $\frac{h}{R} \ll 1$ (error $< 1\%$)
Variation with Depth ($d$ Below Surface)

Inside the Earth at depth $d$, only the concentric sphere of radius $(R - d)$ contributes to the gravitational pull; the outer spherical shell exerts zero net force.

The resulting linear decrease from surface to center is:
Gravity at Depth $d$ (Exact for all $d \le R$)
$$g_d = g \left(1 - \frac{d}{R}\right)$$
  • $d$ : Depth measured from surface downwards $(\text{m})$
  • Center ($d = R$) : $g_{\text{center}} = 0$ (weightlessness at planetary core)
High-Yield Insight: Small Height vs Depth Equivalence

For small heights ($h \ll R$), equating $g_h = g_d$ gives $1 - \frac{2h}{R} = 1 - \frac{d}{R} \implies \mathbf{d = 2h}$.
A decrease in gravity observed at a small height $h$ is duplicated at double the depth ($2h$) below the surface!

Variation with Latitude ($\lambda$) & Earth's Rotation

Because Earth rotates about its polar axis with angular speed $\omega$, objects experience a centrifugal pseudo-force directed radially outward in their local frame.

The effective gravitational acceleration at geographic latitude $\lambda$ is:
Rotational Gravity Variation
$$g' = g - \omega^2 R \cos^2 \lambda$$
  • $\lambda$ : Geographic latitude $(\text{Equator: } \lambda = 0^\circ, \text{Poles: } \lambda = 90^\circ)$
  • $\omega$ : Angular rotation velocity of Earth $(\approx 7.29 \times 10^{-5}\text{ rad/s})$
  • Equator ($\lambda = 0^\circ$) : $g_{\text{eq}} = g - \omega^2 R$ (Minimum gravity)
  • Poles ($\lambda = 90^\circ$) : $g_{\text{pole}} = g$ (Maximum gravity; unaffected by rotation)

3. Gravitational Potential Energy & Escape Velocity

Gravitational force is conservative. Choosing infinity ($r \to \infty$) as the zero reference of potential, the potential energy between two masses is always negative.

The gravitational potential and potential energy at separation distance $r$ are:
Gravitational Potential & Potential Energy
$$V(r) = - \frac{G M}{r} \qquad \text{and} \qquad U(r) = m \cdot V(r) = - \frac{G M m}{r}$$
  • $V(r)$ : Gravitational potential at radial distance $r$ $(\text{J/kg})$
  • $U(r)$ : Gravitational potential energy of two-mass system $(\text{J})$
  • Work Done : To lift mass $m$ from surface to height $h$: $W = \Delta U = \frac{m g h}{1 + \frac{h}{R}}$
Escape Velocity ($v_e$)

Escape velocity is the minimum launch speed required for a projectile to permanently overcome the planetary gravitational field and reach infinity with non-negative kinetic energy.

Applying mechanical energy conservation from surface to infinity yields:
Escape Velocity Formula
$$\frac{1}{2} m v_e^2 - \frac{G M m}{R} = 0 \implies v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2 G M}{R}} = \sqrt{2 g R} \approx 11.2\text{ km/s}$$
  • $v_e$ : Escape velocity from planetary surface $(\approx 11.2\text{ km/s for Earth})$
  • Density Scaling : In terms of density $\rho$: $v_e = R \sqrt{\frac{8\pi G \rho}{3}} \implies v_e \propto R \sqrt{\rho}$
  • Key Property : $v_e$ is completely independent of projectile mass $m$ and launch angle $\theta$!

4. Satellite Dynamics & Kepler's Laws

For a satellite of mass $m$ describing a stable circular orbit of radius $r = R + h$ around Earth, the required centripetal acceleration is provided entirely by the gravitational attraction.

Equating gravitational force to centripetal requirement gives the orbital speed:
Satellite Orbital Speed ($v_o$)
$$\frac{G M m}{r^2} = \frac{m v_o^2}{r} \implies v_o = \sqrt{\frac{G M}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{G M}{R + h}}$$
  • $v_o$ : Orbital velocity $(\text{m/s})$
  • Near-Earth Orbit ($h \approx 0$) : $v_o = \sqrt{g R} \approx 7.92\text{ km/s} = \frac{v_e}{\sqrt{2}}$
Figure: Circular Orbit Dynamics
Orbital Mechanics & Virial Theorem
• Orbital Speed ($v_o$): $$v_o = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R + h}}$$
• Centripetal Force Balance: $$F_g = \frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{m v_o^2}{r}$$
Virial Energy Partitioning: $$\begin{aligned} K &= + \frac{GMm}{2r} \\[4pt] U &= - \frac{GMm}{r} \\[4pt] E_{\text{total}} &= - \frac{GMm}{2r} = - K = \frac{1}{2}U \end{aligned}$$
The time period for one complete revolution obeys Kepler's third law:
Kepler's Third Law (Law of Periods)
$$T = \frac{2\pi r}{v_o} = \frac{2\pi r^{3/2}}{\sqrt{G M}} \implies T^2 = \left(\frac{4\pi^2}{G M}\right) r^3$$
  • $T$ : Orbital period $(\text{s})$
  • $r$ : Semi-major axis or orbital radius $(\text{m})$

Solution 1: Archetype 1: Weight Variation with Height
↑ Back to Question 1
Correct Answer: Option (A) Weight at $h = \frac{R}{2}$ is $32\text{ N}$.
Step 1: State the Exact Height Scaling Relation
Because $h = \frac{R}{2}$ is large compared to $R$ ($50\%$ of radius), the small-height approximation cannot be used. We must apply the exact relation: $$W_h = W \cdot \left(\frac{R}{R + h}\right)^2$$
Step 2: Substitute Parameters & Calculate
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{R}{R + h} &= \frac{R}{R + \frac{R}{2}} = \frac{R}{\frac{3R}{2}} = \frac{2}{3} \\ W_h &= 72 \cdot \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 = 72 \cdot \frac{4}{9} = 32\text{ N} \end{aligned}$$
Solution 2: Archetype 2: Equating Height & Depth Gravity
↑ Back to Question 2
Correct Answer: Option (C) Ratio $\frac{d}{R} = \frac{5}{9}$.
Step 1: Compute Exact Gravity at Height $h = \frac{R}{2}$
$$g_h = g \left(\frac{R}{R + \frac{R}{2}}\right)^2 = g \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{4}{9} g$$
Step 2: Equate with Gravity at Depth $d$
$$\begin{aligned} g_d &= g \left(1 - \frac{d}{R}\right) = \frac{4}{9} g \\ 1 - \frac{d}{R} &= \frac{4}{9} \implies \frac{d}{R} = 1 - \frac{4}{9} = \frac{5}{9} \end{aligned}$$
Solution 3: Archetype 3: Escape Velocity Scaling
↑ Back to Question 3
Correct Answer: Option (B) Escape velocity from planet is $22.4\text{ km/s}$.
Step 1: Write Scaling Proportionality
$$v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2 G M}{R}} \implies v_e \propto \sqrt{\frac{M}{R}}$$
Step 2: Evaluate Planet Escape Speed
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{v_p}{v_e} &= \sqrt{\frac{M_p}{M_e} \cdot \frac{R_e}{R_p}} = \sqrt{2 \cdot \frac{1}{1/2}} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 2} = \sqrt{4} = 2 \\ v_p &= 2 \cdot v_e = 2 \times 11.2\text{ km/s} = 22.4\text{ km/s} \end{aligned}$$

Step 2: Equate and Solve for $d$
$$1 - \frac{2h}{R} = 1 - \frac{d}{R} \implies \frac{d}{R} = \frac{2h}{R} \implies d = 2h$$
Solution 5: Archetype 5: Orbital Speed Scaling

$$v_o = \sqrt{\frac{G M}{r}} \implies v_o \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}$$
Step 2: Compute Velocity Ratio
$$\frac{v_A}{v_B} = \sqrt{\frac{r_B}{r_A}} = \sqrt{\frac{4R}{R}} = \sqrt{4} = 2 \implies \frac{v_A}{v_B} = 2 : 1$$
Solution 6: Archetype 6: Gravitational Potential at Null Point
↑ Back to Question 6
Correct Answer: Option (A) Null point potential is $- \frac{9 G m}{r}$.
Step 1: Locate the Null Field Position
Let the null point be at distance $x$ from mass $m$, so distance from mass $4m$ is $(r - x)$: $$\begin{aligned} \frac{G m}{x^2} &= \frac{G (4m)}{(r - x)^2} \\ \frac{1}{x} &= \frac{2}{r - x} \implies r - x = 2x \implies x = \frac{r}{3} \end{aligned}$$
Step 2: Sum the Scalar Gravitational Potentials
$$\begin{aligned} V &= V_1 + V_2 = - \frac{G m}{x} - \frac{G (4m)}{r - x} \\ &= - \frac{G m}{r / 3} - \frac{4 G m}{2r / 3} = - \frac{3 G m}{r} - \frac{6 G m}{r} = - \frac{9 G m}{r} \end{aligned}$$
Solution 7: Archetype 7: Percentage Change & Error Analysis
↑ Back to Question 7
Correct Answer: Option (A) Surface gravity increases by $2\%$.
Step 1: Write Logarithmic Differential of Gravity
$$g = \frac{G M}{R^2} = G M R^{-2}$$ Taking the natural logarithm and differentiating with constant mass $M$: $$\ln g = \ln(G M) - 2 \ln R \implies \frac{\Delta g}{g} \approx - 2 \frac{\Delta R}{R}$$
Step 2: Substitute Percentage Change
$$\frac{\Delta g}{g} \times 100\% = - 2 \left(- 1\%\right) = + 2\%$$ Because $\Delta g > 0$, surface gravity increases by $2\%$.

6. Key Traps in Gravitation Questions

1. Using Small Height Formula When $h$ is Large

Never use $g_h = g(1 - \frac{2h}{R})$ if $h \ge 0.05 R$ ($320\text{ km}$). For questions like $h = R$ or $h = \frac{R}{2}$, you must use the exact inverse-square formula $g_h = g \frac{R^2}{(R + h)^2}$.

2. Gravitational Potential Energy Sign

Potential energy of a bound system is always negative: $U = - \frac{G M m}{r}$. When a satellite moves to a higher orbit ($r_2 > r_1$), its potential energy increases (becomes less negative), while kinetic energy decreases.

3. Inside Solid Sphere vs Spherical Shell

Inside a uniform solid sphere, gravitational field is linear: $E \propto r$. Inside a thin spherical shell, the gravitational field is strictly zero everywhere ($E = 0$), meaning the gravitational potential inside is constant and equal to surface potential: $V_{\text{inside}} = - \frac{G M}{R}$.

4. Escape Velocity Angle Independence

Escape velocity from surface is $v_e = \sqrt{2 g R}$ regardless of whether the particle is projected vertically, at $30^\circ$, or horizontally (tangentially). Only the radial distance matters for total energy.
