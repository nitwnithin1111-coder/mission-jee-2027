TRAP 01 • VERY COMMON IN JEE 2026
Applying Binomial Approximation for Large Altitude $h$
🛑 What the Student Does (Wrong → -1 Mark)
When a problem asks for $g$ at height $h = R$ or $h = 2R$, student mindlessly writes: $$g' = g \left(1 - \frac{2h}{R}\right) \implies g' = g(1 - 2) = -g \quad \text{(Absurd!)}$$ Or at $h = R/2$, gets $g' = g(1 - 1) = 0$, giving an impossible zero.
✅ What NTA Expects (Right Approach)
The binomial approximation $g_h \approx g\left(1 - \frac{2h}{R}\right)$ is valid ONLY IF $h \ll R$ (typically $h < 320\text{ km}$, i.e., $h < 5\% R$).
For any substantial height (like $h = R, 2R, R/2$), you MUST use the exact formula: $$g_h = \frac{GM}{(R+h)^2} = g \left(\frac{R}{R+h}\right)^2 = \frac{g}{\left(1 + \frac{h}{R}\right)^2}$$ For $h = R$: $g_h = \frac{g}{(1+1)^2} = \frac{g}{4}$.
TRAP 02 • FREQUENT CONFUSION
Equal Reduction in $g$: Height vs Depth Equivalence
🛑 What the Student Does (Wrong)
Equating $g(1 - 2h/R)$ to $g(1 - d/R)$ to conclude $d = 2h$ when $h$ is large ($h = R$).
They state: "At depth $d = 2R$, $g$ is equal to $g$ at $h = R$". (Depth cannot exceed $R$ because Earth's center is at $d = R$!).
✅ What NTA Expects (Right Approach)
1. For small altitude ($h \ll R$): $$g_h = g\left(1 - \frac{2h}{R}\right) \quad \text{and} \quad g_d = g\left(1 - \frac{d}{R}\right) \implies d = 2h$$ A fall in $g$ by $1\%$ at height $h$ requires a depth of $d = 2h$.
2. For finite height (e.g. $h = R$, so $g_h = g/4$): $$g_d = g\left(1 - \frac{d}{R}\right) = \frac{g}{4} \implies 1 - \frac{d}{R} = \frac{1}{4} \implies d = \frac{3}{4}R$$
TRAP 03 • WORK & ENERGY
Assuming Work Done to Raise a Mass is $W = mgh$
🛑 What the Student Does (Wrong)
"Find the minimum work done to lift a mass $m$ to height $h = R$ above Earth's surface."
Student writes: $W = mgh = mgR$. Incorrect!
✅ What NTA Expects (Right Approach)
Because gravity is NOT constant over cosmic distances, work done equals change in potential energy: $$W = \Delta U = U_f - U_i = -\frac{GMm}{R+h} - \left(-\frac{GMm}{R}\right) = GMm \left(\frac{1}{R} - \frac{1}{R+h}\right) = \frac{mgh}{1 + \frac{h}{R}}$$ For $h = R$: $$W = \frac{mgR}{1 + 1} = \frac{1}{2}mgR \quad \text{(Exactly half of } mgR\text{!)}$$
TRAP 04 • ESCAPE VELOCITY
Believing Escape Velocity Depends on the Angle of Projection
🛑 What the Student Does (Wrong)
NTA Question: "A body is projected at an angle of $60^\circ$ to the horizontal. If vertical escape velocity is $v_e$, what is the escape velocity at $60^\circ$?"
Student computes: $v = v_e / \cos(60^\circ) = 2 v_e$ or $v_e \cos(60^\circ)$.
✅ What NTA Expects (Right Approach)
Escape condition comes from total mechanical energy $E \ge 0$: $$\frac{1}{2}m v_e^2 - \frac{GMm}{R} = 0 \implies v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}$$ Kinetic energy $\frac{1}{2}mv^2$ is a pure scalar. As long as the trajectory doesn't collide with the Earth's surface, $v_e$ is strictly independent of projection angle! Answer is always $v_e \approx 11.2\text{ km/s}$.
TRAP 05 • SATELLITE ORBIT SCALING
Confusing Orbital Speed ($v_o$) with Orbital Kinetic Energy or Period
🛑 What the Student Does (Wrong)
"If the orbit radius increases, what happens to speed and kinetic energy?"
Student thinks: "Larger orbit has more total energy, so satellite must move faster ($v \uparrow$)."
✅ What NTA Expects (Right Approach)
Recall the satellite triad: $$v_o = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} \implies \text{As } r \uparrow, \quad v_o \downarrow \quad \text{and} \quad K \downarrow$$ $$U = -\frac{GMm}{r} \implies \text{As } r \uparrow, \quad U \text{ becomes less negative} \implies U \uparrow$$ $$E = -\frac{GMm}{2r} \implies \text{As } r \uparrow, \quad E \uparrow$$ The satellite moves SLOWER in a higher orbit, even though its total mechanical energy is greater!
